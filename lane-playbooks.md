## Rules that apply to every lane {#every-lane}

- **Discovery is triangulated.** Come at each lane at least three ways: (a) direct search with the ICP's vocabulary, (b) follow the audience: where do the personas' known watering holes point (who does this community invite, who does this podcast host follow), (c) competitor shadow: where do the company's competitors and category-adjacent tools show up, get covered, sponsor, guest.
- **Listicles are leads, not conclusions.** "Top 50 B2B podcasts" gives candidates; each still needs an individual fit check against THIS ICP before it earns a row.
- **Every row: a URL you actually fetched.** No fetched URL, no row. Audience numbers need a stated source (platform page, media kit, sponsorship marketplace listing, press page claim) or an explicit "est./unverified".
- **Contact routes are public only.** Listed emails, pitch or booking forms, sponsorship pages, "write for us" pages, public profiles. Never guess an email pattern. If no public route exists, say "no public route, warm intro needed" and note who might bridge.
- **Recency gate.** Prefer entities active in the last 90 days. Something dormant 6+ months goes to Parked or gets dropped, with the reason noted.
- **Cross-pollination.** When an entity spans lanes (a host who also runs a newsletter and a community: one ecosystem, several lanes), research it once, place a row in each lane where a distinct action exists, and cross-reference in Notes.
- **Room-first ethics.** Record each channel's promotion tolerance and design entry plays that respect it. A map that burns the user's reputation in a strict-ban room is worse than no map.
- **Anti-ICP filter.** A huge audience of the wrong people scores Fit = 1. Check who the audience actually is, not how big it is.
- If a Reddit search or archive tool is available, use it for subreddit discovery and for evidence of which rooms, shows, and newsletters the ICP actually cites ("find the rooms").

## Journalists {#journalists}

Named individual reporters, editors, and columnists who cover the company's category or its buyers' world. This lane is people, not outlets; outlets go in Publications.

- **Discover:** search recent (12 months or less) coverage of the category, competitors, and the ICP's pain vocabulary; check bylines on the 10 most relevant stories per major outlet; outlet staff and masthead pages; journalists' own public profiles (LinkedIn, X, personal sites, public media-database profiles); who covered competitors' funding and launches.
- **Record:** name, outlet(s), beat as THEY describe it, 2-3 linked recent relevant pieces with dates, what kind of story they actually write (funding, product, trend, contrarian op-ed, data-driven), pitch angle tailored to their beat, public contact route, freelance vs. staff.
- **Verify:** most recent byline 90 days old or less; still at the outlet (mastheads rot: journalists move constantly); the linked pieces genuinely match the beat claim.
- **Entry plays:** data-first pitch (original numbers beat opinions), trend piece with the company as one source among several, founder-as-expert commentary on their live story thread, respond to public callouts for sources.
- **Trap:** a journalist who covered the space once, 18 months ago, is not a beat reporter. Two or more relevant pieces in 12 months or they're Tier B at best.

## Publications {#publications}

Outlets as entities: trade press, business press sections, contributed-content programs, sector verticals.

- **Discover:** where competitors got covered; trade press per ICP vertical; contributed-content programs ("write for us", councils, expert networks); the outlets the personas cite in their own posts.
- **Record:** type (trade / business / vertical / contributed program), audience relevance in one sentence, story angle matched to their actual coverage, entry route (earned pitch / contributed / paid program with cost), submission guidelines link.
- **Verify:** section still publishing (last article date); contributed program still open; council or program fees current.
- **Entry plays:** earned first with contributed as fallback; op-ed exchanges; data partnerships (give them an exclusive stat).
- **Trap:** pay-to-play councils look like earned media but read as ads to sophisticated buyers. Record the cost and label honestly.

## Podcasts {#podcasts}

- **Discover:** chart categories matching ICP interests; guest-trail the competitors and adjacent founders (where did they appear); host cross-references (good hosts guest on each other's shows); podcast search engines and the shows' own guest pages.
- **Record:** show, host (a person, often a Creators-lane row too), audience signal (chart position, download claims, review count, with source), format and episode length, guest vs. sponsor economics, the specific pitch angle for THIS show's audience, booking route, landing difficulty (easy / medium / hard).
- **Verify:** episode shipped 60 days ago or less; host still the host (rebrand trap: shows rename and change hosts); sponsorship rates only from public rate cards or credible reports.
- **Entry plays:** guest beats sponsor at early stage; a specific episode-title-shaped pitch beats a bio blast; offer original data or a war story, not a product tour.
- **Trap:** download numbers are self-reported and inflated; prefer relative signals (chart position, review velocity, guest caliber).

## Newsletters {#newsletters}

- **Discover:** sponsorship marketplaces (public listings give roughly audited sizes and rates); who the personas quote and forward; cross-promo networks; Substack category leaderboards.
- **Record:** name, author/publisher, subscriber count with source, open-rate claim if public, sponsorship route and rate where public (tag rate confidence separately), non-paid entry plays (get quoted, get listed in their recommendations, tool roundups), CPM math where the rate is known.
- **Verify:** issue shipped 30 days ago or less (newsletters die quietly); rate from a listed source, else "rate unknown".
- **Entry plays:** cheapest quality-CPM buys as anchors; earn a mention via original data; a spot on a newsletter's recommended-vendor or resource list is often worth more than one sponsorship.
- **Trap:** subscriber counts on the author's own about page are marketing; marketplace-listed numbers are closer to real. Note which you have.

## Communities {#communities}

Slack and Discord groups, paid memberships, subreddits, forums, peer networks.

- **Discover:** "community" + ICP title or vertical searches; where personas say they hang out (bios, podcast intros); subreddit search by pain vocabulary; peer-network directories; vendor-run communities (a vendor hosting a peer group for the buyer title).
- **Record:** name, type (free open / free vetted / paid / subreddit), size with source, who's actually in it (titles), **promotion tolerance: open / gray / strict-ban** (this field is load-bearing), an entry play that respects the tolerance, cost to join or sponsor.
- **Verify:** activity level (recent posts or threads visible from outside, member-count recency); vetting criteria: can a vendor even get in?
- **Entry plays:** strict-ban rooms: cultivate member-customers who talk; gray rooms: genuinely useful answers with disclosure; open rooms: sponsorships and AMAs; subreddits: a specialist answering the recurring threads where buyers ask whether the problem is worth paying to solve.
- **Trap:** size is inversely correlated with value. A 200-person vetted room of exact-ICP titles outranks a subreddit with millions. Score Fit accordingly.

## Creators: LinkedIn {#creators-linkedin}

Operators and voices whose followers match the ICP.

- **Discover:** who the personas' watering holes platform (community founders, podcast hosts); who competitors' posts get engagement from; who writes about the ICP's pain; "top voices" lists as leads only.
- **Record:** name, role and credibility (why the audience trusts them), follower count plus engagement quality (comments from ICP titles beat raw likes), owned assets (newsletter, community, course: cross-reference), collab angle, **alignment risk** (do they preach something that contradicts the pitch? a creator who argues for the very approach your product replaces is a risky partner, even with a perfect audience), cost signal ($/$$/$$$ or known rate).
- **Verify:** posted 30 days ago or less; audience composition by sampling commenters' titles, not by follower count.
- **Entry plays:** paid partnerships with the 1-2 highest-fit anchors; earned mentions via original data they'd want to cite; podcast-guest swaps; comment-section presence by the founder (free, compounding).
- **Trap:** big-follower generalists convert worse than mid-size specialists whose comment sections are full of buyers.

## Creators: YouTube {#creators-youtube}

- **Discover:** search the ICP's how-to and comparison queries on YouTube; channels reviewing or teaching the adjacent stack; competitor integrations and sponsor reads; conference-talk channels.
- **Record:** channel, format (interview / tutorial / essay / review), subscriber count plus median recent views (views matter more), audience evidence (comments, who they interview), play type: integration read vs. dedicated video vs. founder as guest, sponsorship route and rate if public.
- **Verify:** upload 60 days ago or less; median views on the last 10 videos (subscriber counts fossilize).
- **Entry plays:** guest on interview channels (free, evergreen, and it feeds GEO: YouTube transcripts get cited by LLMs); tool-comparison inclusion; sponsor only where median views x ICP share beats newsletter CPM.
- **Trap:** B2B YouTube is thin in most niches. A short honest lane beats a padded one. Say when the lane is thin.

## Creators: X {#creators-x}

- **Discover:** who the founder persona actually follows and replies to; who breaks category news; who quote-posts competitors' announcements; relevant recurring conversations in the category (build in public, GTM, "this category is broken" discourse).
- **Record:** handle, follower count, what they actually engage with (sampled from the last ~50 posts), play type: earned mention / reply presence / paid, thread vs. one-liner vs. essay style, cross-platform assets.
- **Verify:** active 14 days ago or less (X accounts go dormant fast); engagement real (ratio of replies from real accounts).
- **Entry plays:** the founder account replying with substance to the right 15 accounts (free, compounding); original-data threads they'd quote; paid only for proven-fit accounts with public sponsorship offers.
- **Trap:** X reach is founder-brand reach. Plays here mostly need the CEO's voice, not the company account. Flag that dependency per row.

## Events {#events}

- **Discover:** per-vertical conference calendars; where competitors sponsor and speak; community-run events; exec dinner circuits (peer-group chapters, executive roundtables); CFP (call for papers) listings.
- **Record:** name, subtype (major conference / boutique / dinner / meetup / virtual), next date and location (real, verified), audience size and composition, sold-stage vs. earned-stage vs. attend-and-hallway play, cost-effort, CFP deadline if speaking is the play.
- **Verify:** next edition actually announced with a date (zombie events abound); ticket and sponsor pricing from the event site.
- **Entry plays:** earned stages via CFP with a data talk; boutique-conference density over major-conference badge scans; the "2-3 hustlers on the ground" play at high-density events; host a dinner adjacent to a major conference instead of sponsoring it.
- **Trap:** timing is structural. A great event 11 months out is an M; a good-enough one in 6 weeks with an open CFP is an H. Score with the calendar, and mind the seasonal crunches (in many B2B markets, Sept-Nov and Apr-Jun).

## Analysts, Awards & Review sites {#analysts-awards-reviews}

The credibility lane: third-party validation surfaces that sales cycles and AI answers both cite.

- **Discover:** which analyst firms and NAMED analysts cover the category (large-firm coverage areas, boutique analysts, industry newsletters that act as analysts); category awards with real selectivity; review platforms and the exact category pages buyers land on.
- **Record:** entity, type (analyst firm / named analyst / award / review platform category), why it matters for THIS ICP (procurement checklists? AI-answer citations?), engagement route (briefing request, award application with deadline and fee, review-generation program), cost, deadline where applicable.
- **Verify:** analyst still covers the beat (check recent publications); the award's most recent cycle actually took place; the review category page is the one with traffic (check which category competitors invest in).
- **Entry plays:** free analyst briefings (most boutique and many big-firm analysts take vendor briefings at no cost); awards with deadlines land in the Shortlist with dates; review-velocity programs on the ONE category page that matters.
- **Trap:** pay-to-win awards are ad spend wearing a medal. Record the fee and label honestly. Analyst relations is a long game; mark realistic time-to-impact.

## Partnerships & Affiliates {#partnerships-affiliates}

Distribution through other companies' audiences.

- **Discover:** marketplaces and app directories where the ICP already shops (per vertical); integration ecosystems adjacent to the product; VC platform teams (portfolio perks and deal pages); agencies, consultants, and fractional execs who advise the ICP and could refer; affiliate and rev-share programs of complementary tools; co-marketing candidates (non-competing tools sharing the ICP).
- **Record:** partner, type (marketplace / integration / VC platform / referral network / affiliate / co-marketing), audience overlap logic, what the partner gets (the deal must work for them: state it), entry route and named team where public (platform or partnerships pages), effort-to-live.
- **Verify:** program actually open (partnership pages rot); marketplace listing requirements current.
- **Entry plays:** VC platform one-pager plus a portfolio success story; listed-integration launches with a co-announcement; a referral kit for fractional execs and consultants; co-webinar with a complementary tool's audience.
- **Trap:** partnerships are slow-burn. Most rows here are M/L priority with 1-2 H exceptions where a warm path already exists. Don't let this lane crowd the Shortlist.

## GEO {#geo}

The AI-answer battleground: which buyer queries matter, who wins them today, what changes the answer.

- **Discover:** build the query set from the ICP's buying journey (category queries, "best X", pricing queries, "alternatives to [incumbent]", "is [pain] worth solving" queries, comparison queries); then RUN the queries where tooling allows (web search previews, AI answer engines) and record who's cited.
- **Record:** query or surface, buyer intent (high/med/low), who wins it today (named, with URL evidence of the citing sources), which sources the answer engines actually cite (those sources become pitch targets in other lanes: cross-reference), what the company must do to enter the answer, effort.
- **Verify:** actually run the query this run. GEO answers move monthly; a quarter-old citation claim is stale. Date-stamp every observation.
- **Entry plays:** own the pricing or benchmark query with citable, concrete data (LLMs prefer specific numbers over vague ranges); get into the 2-3 listicles LLMs keep citing (pay-to-earn where honest); publish an original-data study built to be cited; win the review-site category page that answers feed on.
- **Trap:** GEO wins route through OTHER lanes (the outlets, listicles, and review pages that answers cite). This tab must cross-reference, not duplicate; its unique value is the query, who-wins, what-moves-it chain.
